AI at Work

AI for office staff and clerks: letters, minutes, emails and Excel

Office work is full of text that follows a pattern. That is exactly what AI drafts well, when you give it the facts and read the result.

Where it helps

  • Letters and notices from a few points: leave application replies, reminders, circulars, quotations.
  • Minutes of meeting from your own rough notes.
  • Replies to emails, shorter or more polite or more firm.
  • Translating a letter between English and Hindi or Punjabi, then having it read by someone fluent.
  • Spreadsheet help: formulas, cleaning names and dates, merging lists (test on a copy).
  • Checklists and standard procedures for recurring tasks.

A safe workflow

  1. Strip the private details from your notes: replace names with roles, remove numbers and addresses.
  2. Write the prompt with task, audience, format and limits (see the prompt page).
  3. Read the draft line by line. Check every name, date and number.
  4. Put the real details back yourself.
  5. Show it to your boss if it will go outside the office.
  6. Keep the final version in your usual system, not in the chat.

Prompts you can adapt

Letter. "Write a formal letter in English from the office of a small school to a parent, informing them that the fee instalment is due on [date] and that a late fee applies after [date]. Polite, firm, under 120 words. Leave the placeholders in square brackets."

Minutes. "Turn these rough notes into minutes with headings: attendees, points discussed, decisions, action items with owner and date. Do not add anything that is not in the notes. Notes: [paste notes without private details]."

Email tone. "Rewrite this email to be polite but clear that the deadline cannot move. Keep it under 80 words. Email: [paste]."

Clean a list. "I have a column of names in the form 'RAO, ASHA' and I want 'Asha Rao'. Give me an Excel formula that converts one cell, and explain it in two lines."

Things not to do

  • Do not paste salary lists, personal files, passwords or confidential contracts.
  • Do not send a draft that you have not read.
  • Do not let AI invent policy: if the notice quotes a rule, take it from the official policy document.
  • Do not use your personal account for confidential office data if the office has rules. Ask.

Hindi and Punjabi

Ask for Devanagari or Gurmukhi script and simple words, and have a fluent colleague read the result (see the page on Hindi and Punjabi).

Build your own library

Keep a document with the prompts that worked, one per recurring task, and the settings you used. The prompt library page gives a starting set.

Will AI make my job unnecessary?

Nobody can promise. People who use it well and check its work are more useful than people who either ignore it or trust it blindly.

Which tool should I use?

The one your office permits. See the page on choosing a tool.
