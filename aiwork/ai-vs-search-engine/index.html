AI chatbot or search engine: which to use for what

Both are useful, for different jobs. Choosing the right one saves time and avoids errors.

The difference in one line

A search engine finds pages that already exist and shows you where they are. A chatbot writes a new answer. Some tools do both: they search the web and then write a reply with links, which you should open.

A decision table

You want to...Better choiceWhy
Find the official rule, form or noticeSearch engine, then the official siteYou need the source itself, and its date
Check today's price, news or weatherSearch engineA chatbot may not know recent facts
Draft a letter, email or noticeChatbotIt writes text on request
Understand a concept in simple wordsChatbot, then verifyIt can adapt the explanation to you
Find a specific person, shop or addressSearch engine or mapsNames and addresses are where chatbots invent
Rewrite or shorten your own textChatbotYou provide the facts
Learn what a section of a law saysOfficial text, then chatbot to explain itDo not rely on the chatbot's memory of the law
Compare productsBothChatbot to organise your questions, search for current specifications
Fix an error messageBothChatbot for ideas, search for the exact error and official documentation
Get a medical or legal opinionNeither aloneAsk a qualified person

A good habit: search first for facts, chatbot for words

Use search to collect the facts from reliable pages. Paste the key facts into the chatbot and ask it to write or explain using only those facts. That gives you the speed of a chatbot with the reliability of the sources.

When a chatbot has web search

Some tools can search and cite. It helps, but it does not remove the need to check: the tool may pick a poor source, misread a page or merge two sources. Open the cited page and look for the sentence.

Privacy differences

When you search, your query goes to the search company. When you use a chatbot you often send far more text: whole documents and conversations. The privacy pages on this site explain how to limit this.

Cost and speed

Search is fast and free (with ads). A chatbot reply can take longer and may have usage limits on the free plan. If you need many quick factual checks, search wins.

Is a chatbot better than Google now?

They do different jobs. For facts that must be exact and current, search and the original source win.

Can I trust a chatbot's links?

Open them. Sometimes the link is real but does not say what the chatbot claims; sometimes it does not exist.
