AI for accountants and tax professionals: safe uses, prompts and cautions

AI can save an accountant hours on drafting and explaining. It must never be the source of a rate, a section number or a client's figures.

Good uses

TaskWhat AI is good atWhat you must do
Drafting a reply to a notice or letterStructure, polite firm tone, headingsAdd the facts, section numbers and dates from the official source
Client emails and remindersClear short messages in English or HindiRemove client identifiers before you ask, fill them in after
Explaining a concept to a clientPlain-language explanations, analogiesCheck the rule against the official text
Variance and trend commentaryTurning your numbers into sentencesGive it figures you have checked, and check the sentences
Spreadsheet formulasSuggesting a formula and explaining itTest it on sample data (see the Excel page)
Checklists and proceduresFirst draft of a month-end or audit checklistReview with your own standards
Summarising a long circularA short summary and questions to askRead the circular itself for anything you act on

What never to do

  • Never take a rate, threshold, deadline or section number from the tool without checking the official notification, gazette or portal. This is exactly the sort of detail that tools invent or remember from an older year.
  • Never paste a client's PAN, Aadhaar, bank details, GSTIN with business data, or a full ledger into a consumer AI tool. See the checklist and the DPDP page.
  • Never file anything that you have not read.

Prompts you can adapt

Explain a variance. "Here are monthly totals for 'Repairs' for the last six months: 12, 14, 13, 41, 15, 14 (in thousands of rupees). Write three possible reasons for the spike in month four that an accountant should ask the client about. Do not state any as fact."

Draft a reply outline. "I need to reply to a notice that says the input tax credit claimed differs from the credit shown in the supplier-matched statement. Write an outline of a reply with these headings: reference, summary of the notice, our position, documents attached, request. Leave placeholders in square brackets for facts and section numbers. Do not invent any legal references."

Client reminder. "Write a polite WhatsApp message in simple English to a client reminding them to send purchase bills for the month by the 5th. Under 50 words, friendly, no pressure."

Checklist. "Make a month-end checklist for a small trading company's books: bank reconciliation, stock, advances, accruals, payroll. Group by task, one line each."

Keep a record

Note which parts of a work product came from AI and who checked them. If a regulator or client asks how you prepared something, you can answer honestly.

Professional rules

Your institute's code of conduct, your engagement letters and your clients' expectations come first. If your institute or firm has an AI policy, follow it. If it does not, write one (the template page can start you).

Can AI do my GST return?

Software that connects to your books and the portal prepares returns. A chat tool writes text. Use the right tool, and check every figure.

Is it fine to use AI for client letters?

Yes for drafting, if you remove identifiers and read the final letter. The responsibility is yours.
