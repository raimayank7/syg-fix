Which AI tool for which job: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot

There is no single best tool, and the ranking changes every few months. Choose by your job and your habits, then test with your own task.

Why we do not name a winner

Chatbots are updated constantly: new models, new features, new limits. A comparison written today is out of date within months, and the companies' own pages claim improvements all the time. So this guide gives a method, not a ranking.

Choose by the place you work

If you already live in...Look first atBecause
Microsoft 365 (Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams)Microsoft CopilotMicrosoft's help page describes Copilot as integrated into Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams, working with your files. Some features depend on your subscription
Google (Gmail, Docs, Drive, Android)GeminiIt works with Google's apps when you allow it
Neither, or you want a standalone assistantChatGPT or ClaudeBoth are general chat assistants with apps for web, phone and desktop

Choose by the task

  • Writing and editing text: try two tools with the same draft and your own prompt. Compare tone, accuracy and how much you must change.
  • Spreadsheets: check whether the tool can open your file and show the formulas, and always test the formulas it writes (see the page on Excel formulas).
  • Documents and long files: check the size of file it accepts, whether it keeps your formatting, and what it does with your data.
  • Images: see the page on image tools. Not every chatbot makes images.
  • Hindi or Punjabi: test with a text that you can judge.
  • Research: prefer a tool that shows sources and open them.

Choose by privacy

Read the privacy page for each tool you plan to use: how long chats are kept, whether people review them, whether they are used to train models, and how to turn that off. This site has such a page for ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, and a comparison. For client or student data, use only what your workplace permits.

A one-hour test

  1. Pick three real tasks from your week (an email, a spreadsheet question, a summary).
  2. Run each in two tools with identical prompts.
  3. Score the answers on accuracy, usefulness and edits needed.
  4. Check one fact in each answer against a source.
  5. Choose the tool that needs the fewest corrections for your work, and check its privacy settings.

Free before paid

Start with the free plans. Pay only when you hit a limit that costs you time. See the page on free and paid plans.

Re-test now and then

Every few months run the same three tasks again. Tools change, and so may your needs.

Which is the most accurate?

No tool is always right. Accuracy depends on the task. Use the one-hour test with your own work.

Can I use more than one?

Yes. Many people use two: one for drafting and one for checking or for a second opinion.
