AI at Work

AI for teachers: lesson plans, worksheets and feedback, with limits

Used well, AI is a tireless assistant for the preparation that eats evenings. It does not replace your knowledge of your class.

What helps most

  • First drafts of lesson plans with timings, activities and questions.
  • Practice questions at several levels, from easy to challenging, for the same topic.
  • Explanations in different words for students who did not follow the first one.
  • Rubrics and feedback comments that you adapt.
  • Parent messages and notices, in English or Hindi/Punjabi, which you read before sending.
  • Ideas for activities, games and real-life examples.

Prompts you can adapt

Lesson plan. "Make a 40-minute lesson plan for class 7 science on the difference between a physical and a chemical change. Include a 5-minute starter question, one demonstration using materials found in a school lab or kitchen, two worked examples, and a 3-question exit quiz. Use simple English. Add a note on any safety precaution."

Differentiated questions. "Write 9 questions on multiplying decimals for class 6: three easy (one step), three medium (two steps) and three challenging (word problem). Give the answers separately at the end."

Explain differently. "Explain photosynthesis to a class 5 student in six sentences, using the example of a kitchen. Then explain the same idea in four sentences for a class 9 student."

Feedback. "Here is a paragraph written by a class 8 student [paste without the name]. Write three kind comments: what is good, what to improve, one next step. Under 60 words."

What you must check

  • Accuracy. Check facts, formulas and answers, in particular in science and mathematics. Answers to the questions it writes can be wrong: solve them yourself.
  • Syllabus fit. The tool does not know your board's current syllabus or textbook. Compare with the book.
  • Level and language. Read for difficulty and for words your students know.
  • Bias and sensitivity. Names, examples and pictures should suit your students.
  • Safety. For experiments, check against your school's rules, not the tool.

Student data

Do not paste students' names with marks, health, family or behaviour details into a public AI tool. Remove names. Follow your school's rules. See the checklist page and the page on India's data protection law.

Students using AI

Tell students your rules: when AI is allowed, how to say that they used it, and why it matters that they understand the work. Design tasks that need their own thinking: explain in class, use a local example, show steps. Do not rely on "AI detectors": they make mistakes and can accuse an honest student.

Time saved, quality kept

The saving is largest when you give a clear prompt and edit the result. Keep your good prompts in a document and reuse them.

Can AI mark essays?

It can give feedback comments to a draft. Grades with real consequences should be yours.

Is it fair that I use AI and students cannot?

Be open about how you use it and set clear rules for them. Many schools write a policy for both.
