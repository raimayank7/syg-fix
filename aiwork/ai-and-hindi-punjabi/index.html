Using AI in Hindi and Punjabi: what works and what to check

AI tools can write and understand Hindi and Punjabi, but quality varies with the tool, the topic and the script. A few habits help.

What to expect

Most large AI tools can read and write Hindi, and many can handle Punjabi in Gurmukhi. Typical results: good Hindi for everyday letters, messages and explanations; weaker results for formal Punjabi, local sayings, technical terms and mixed-language writing. Quality changes between tools and between versions, so test the tool you use on a text that you can judge yourself before you rely on it. We have not tested individual tools for this page, and the advice is general.

Habits that improve results

  1. Say the language and script. "Reply in Hindi, in Devanagari" or "Punjabi in Gurmukhi script". Otherwise a tool may answer in English or in roman letters.
  2. Say the register. "Simple spoken Hindi that a shopkeeper would use", "formal letter language", or "friendly message".
  3. Write the prompt in the language if you can. A prompt in English asking for Hindi output is fine; a prompt in Hindi may give more natural output.
  4. Give an example of the style that you like.
  5. Keep sentences short in the request and ask for short sentences in the answer.
  6. Keep technical words in English if people use them that way: GST, invoice, WhatsApp, password. Say so: "Keep English words that people commonly use."
  7. Ask for both: a Hindi text and an English meaning line, so you can check what it says.

What to check every time

  • Names, places and numbers. The tool may change a spelling or turn digits into other forms.
  • Meaning. Fluent words can still say something slightly different from what you meant, in particular with legal or financial wording.
  • Tone. A phrase can sound too formal, too casual or rude without being wrong.
  • Script problems. Look for broken letters, missing vowel signs and wrong conjuncts, especially in Punjabi.
  • Cultural fit. Greetings, idioms and festival wishes should suit your audience.

Have a native reader look at anything public

If the text will be printed, posted, sent to customers or used in school, ask a person who reads the language well to look at it. The tool saves you the writing time; the person saves you from embarrassment.

Translation tips

  • Translate paragraph by paragraph, not a whole document at once.
  • Translate back to English in a separate chat, and compare with your original.
  • For documents with legal effect, use a qualified translator.

Voice and speech

Speech tools (voice typing and read-aloud) are separate features with their own quality in each language. Test them with your own accent before you rely on them.

Why does the tool answer in English when I write in Hindi?

Tell it the language you want for the answer. Put it in the first line of the prompt.

Can I use roman Hindi (Hinglish)?

Usually yes for chat. For printed material ask for Devanagari and check it.
