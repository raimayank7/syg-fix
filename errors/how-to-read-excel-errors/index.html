Excel error values: what each one means

Each Excel error starts with a hash sign and names a kind of problem. Knowing the kind tells you where to look.

The error values at a glance

ErrorThe formula...First thing to check
#DIV/0!divides by zero or by an empty cellThe divisor cell
#N/Alooked something up and did not find itThe value and the list: spaces, text versus numbers
#NAME?contains a word Excel does not knowSpelling of functions and names, missing quotes
#VALUE!has the wrong type of valueText where a number is needed
#REF!points at a cell that was deletedThe formula text: the broken part reads #REF!
#NUM!has a number that is not allowedNegative roots, impossible results
#NULL!uses a space between ranges that do not meetA space where a comma belongs
#SPILL!cannot show all of its resultsCells in the way below or beside it
#CALC!returned an empty arrayThe condition of FILTER; add a third argument
#####is fine, but the column is too narrowWiden the column

Each of these has its own page on this site. The examples there were typed into a real copy of Excel, and the results shown are what Excel displayed.

Tools that find the cause

  • Evaluate Formula (Formulas tab) steps through a formula and shows the value of each part, until you see where it turns into an error.
  • Trace Precedents and Trace Dependents draw arrows to the cells that a formula uses, and to the cells that use it.
  • Error Checking (Formulas tab) goes through the sheet and explains each error in words.
  • F9 in the formula bar: select a part of a formula and press F9 to see its value. Press Esc to leave the formula unchanged.
  • The little warning icon beside an error cell has a menu with "Help on this error" and "Show Calculation Steps".

Do not hide errors too early

IFERROR(formula, 0) hides every error, including the ones that tell you the formula is wrong. Fix the cause first. Use IFNA for a missing lookup, and IF for the cases that you expect, such as an empty divisor. Reserve IFERROR for places where you really do not care why the formula failed.

Types and spaces cause most problems

A large share of #N/A, #VALUE! and #DIV/0! errors come from data: numbers stored as text, spaces at the end of words, and cells that look empty but are not. Check with =ISNUMBER(A1), =LEN(A1) and =TRIM(A1), and clean the data once.

Do errors spread to other cells?

Yes. A formula that uses a cell with an error usually shows the same error. Fix the first one and the others disappear.

Why is a hash in a cell not an error value?

It only says the column is too narrow to show the content. The cell value itself is fine.
