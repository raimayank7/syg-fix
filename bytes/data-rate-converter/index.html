Data rate converter: Mbps to MB/s and back

Internet providers use bits per second. Download programs show bytes per second. This tool converts between them.

The one rule

There are 8 bits in a byte. To change megabits per second (Mbit/s, often written Mbps) into megabytes per second (MB/s), divide by 8. A 100 Mbit/s connection can move at most 12.5 MB/s, and a 1 Gbit/s connection at most 125 MB/s.

What the table shows

For the speed that you type, the table lists it in bits per second (bit/s, kbit/s, Mbit/s, Gbit/s) and in bytes per second (B/s, KB/s, MB/s, GB/s) with decimal prefixes, and in the binary units (KiB/s, MiB/s, GiB/s). Providers use decimal prefixes: 1 Mbit/s is 1,000,000 bit/s.

Why real downloads are slower than the line speed

  • Overhead. Every packet carries headers for addresses and error checking.
  • The server. A slow server cannot fill a fast line.
  • Wi-Fi. The signal weakens with distance and walls, and many devices share it.
  • Sharing. Other people and devices in your home use the same connection.
  • Distance and routing. Data far away takes longer to arrive and slows down some transfers.

A fair allowance is 80 to 90 percent of the line speed on a good wired connection, and less on Wi-Fi.

Common mistakes

  1. Expecting 100 MB/s from a 100 Mbit/s line (it is 12.5).
  2. Comparing a provider's Mbit/s with a download window's MB/s without converting.
  3. Forgetting that upload speed is often much lower than download speed.
Is Mbps the same as Mbit/s?

Yes in connection speeds. "Mbps" and "Mb/s" both mean megabits per second.

What is MiB/s?

Mebibytes per second: 1,048,576 bytes per second.
