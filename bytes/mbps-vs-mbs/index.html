Mbps vs MB/s: how to read your internet speed

Mbps is megabits per second and MB/s is megabytes per second. They differ by a factor of eight.

The letters

In Mbps, the b is for bits. In MB/s (or MBps), the B is for bytes. Your provider sells Mbps. Your browser, phone and download programs often show MB/s. One byte is eight bits, so divide Mbps by 8 to get MB/s.

Reading a speed test

A speed test shows download and upload speeds in Mbps, and often a "ping" or latency in milliseconds. Run it with a cable or close to the router, with other devices idle, and run it a few times. If the result is much lower than your plan, check your Wi-Fi first, then the router, then your provider's own line test.

Why you rarely see the full speed

Plans are described "up to" a speed. Overhead, Wi-Fi, the distance to the server, busy hours and the quality of the line all reduce it. If you get 80 to 90 percent on a cable, that is normal.

Upload and download

Many plans have a lower upload than download speed. Uploading a big file (a video to a cloud service) takes longer than downloading one of the same size.

Latency is a different number

Latency is the delay for a message to go and return, in milliseconds. A line can have high speed and high latency (a satellite link, for example). Latency matters for calls and games more than for downloads.

Common mistakes

  1. Comparing Mbps and MB/s without converting.
  2. Testing over Wi-Fi from another room and blaming the provider.
  3. Expecting the speed test result at all hours.
What speed do I need?

For video calls and streaming in high definition, 10 to 25 Mbps per user is typically enough; heavy households need more. Check your services' requirements.

Is Gbps the same as GB/s?

No. Gbps is gigabits per second. GB/s is gigabytes per second, eight times more.
