How long does a download take? A table of sizes and speeds

Time equals size divided by speed, after you convert bytes to bits. This table has the answers for common cases.

How to adjust it

Real downloads reach perhaps 80 to 90 percent of the line speed on a good cable, and less over Wi-Fi. Divide the best-case time by 0.85 for a realistic estimate on a cable: 10 minutes becomes about 12 minutes.

The formula

Time in seconds = size in bytes x 8 divided by speed in bits per second. For 4.7 GB at 100 Mbit/s: 4.7 x 10^9 x 8 / 100 x 10^6 = 376 seconds.

Planning big transfers

  • Overnight. If the time is hours, start the transfer in the evening.
  • A cable, not Wi-Fi. It makes the biggest difference.
  • Check data limits. Some plans slow down after a monthly quota.
  • Use a program that resumes interrupted downloads.
  • Check your upload speed if you are sending, because it is often slower.

Common mistakes

  1. Using Mbit/s as if it were MB/s.
  2. Ignoring the allowance for real-world overhead.
  3. Not checking whether your plan's data limit allows the download.
