How to compare AI models by cost

A price per million tokens means little until you put your own task into it.

Read on 14 November 2026. Prices and limits change: check the source.

Steps

  1. Measure a typical request. How many tokens go in (instructions, documents, history) and how many come out? The token estimator in this umbrella gives a range, and the makers' own counters give exact numbers.
  2. Use both prices. Cost = input tokens ÷ 1,000,000 × input price + output tokens ÷ 1,000,000 × output price.
  3. Multiply by requests per day or month, with a margin.
  4. Check the details of the price. Dated changes, higher prices for long prompts, separate prices for audio or images, and batch discounts all change the result.
  5. Test quality yourself. A cheaper model that gets your task wrong costs more than a dearer one that gets it right. This site gives no quality scores.

Things the price list does not tell you

  • How well the model does your task.
  • How fast it answers.
  • Rate limits and availability in your country.
  • Terms about what the maker does with your data. Read them for private data.
Should I always choose the cheapest model?

No. Choose the cheapest one that passes your own tests.
