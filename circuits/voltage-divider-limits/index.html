Voltage divider limits and loading

A divider is simple, but it assumes that nothing takes current from the output.

What happens with a load

The output of a divider is across R2. A load resistor connected to the output is in parallel with R2, which makes the lower part smaller and the output lower. The calculated values below are for a 5 V input.

Load on the output (R1 = R2 = 10 kΩ, 5 V in)Output voltage
No load2.5 V
1 MΩ2.488 V
100 kΩ2.381 V
10 kΩ1.667 V
1 kΩ0.4167 V

Rules of thumb

  1. Make the load at least ten times larger than R2 (a hundred times if you need accuracy).
  2. A lower resistance keeps the output steadier under load, but wastes more current (V² / (R1 + R2) is always flowing).
  3. Use a buffer or a regulator when the load is not tiny.

What a divider is good for

Dividers are good for scaling a voltage to read it, for example in front of an analog input, and for setting a reference where nothing is drawn. They are not for power: use a regulator.

How do I get exactly 3.3 V from 5 V?

Use a regulator. A divider gives the value that the standard resistors allow and changes with load.

Do the resistor tolerances matter?

Yes: the output moves by about the sum of the tolerances. Use 1% parts for accuracy.
