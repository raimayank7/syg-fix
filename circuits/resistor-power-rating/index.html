Resistor power rating

A resistor turns electrical energy into heat. If it gets more power than its rating, it gets hot and may burn.

Calculate the power

P = V² / R = I² × R = V × I. Use whichever pair of values you know.

Choosing the rating

A common rule of thumb is to choose a resistor rated for at least twice the calculated power, which leaves room for heat, for higher temperatures in a box and for a voltage that is a little higher than planned. Common ratings of small resistors are 1/8 W, 1/4 W, 1/2 W and 1 W; larger ones are wire-wound or chassis types.

Watch the heat

A resistor running near its rating gets hot to the touch, which is normal for its design but can discolour a circuit board or damage nearby parts. Leave space around power resistors and do not enclose them tightly.

Data sheets

The rating is given at a stated ambient temperature, and drops at higher temperatures (a derating curve). Read the data sheet of the part for high-power or hot designs.

Does the resistor value change when it gets hot?

Yes, by its temperature coefficient (the sixth colour band, where present).

Can I use two resistors to share the power?

Yes: two equal resistors in series or in parallel share the power equally.
