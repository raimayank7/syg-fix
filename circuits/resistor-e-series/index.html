E12 and E24 resistor values

You cannot buy a 4,600 ohm resistor in a normal shop. Resistor values come in steps.

The idea

Resistors are made with a tolerance, such as ±5% or ±10%. A series of values is chosen so that each value, with its tolerance, nearly touches the next one. A finer tolerance needs more values per decade. The E12 series has 12 values per decade (for ±10%) and E24 has 24 (for ±5%).

The values

SeriesValues in one decade (1 to 10)Tolerance it is made for
E121, 1.2, 1.5, 1.8, 2.2, 2.7, 3.3, 3.9, 4.7, 5.6, 6.8, 8.2about ±10%
E241, 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.5, 1.6, 1.8, 2, 2.2, 2.4, 2.7, 3, 3.3, 3.6, 3.9, 4.3, 4.7, 5.1, 5.6, 6.2, 6.8, 7.5, 8.2, 9.1about ±5%

Each list repeats in every power of ten: 10, 100, 1 k and 10 k, and 12, 120, 1.2 k, and so on. E12 is contained in E24.

About the source

These are the values printed in common references. The standard that defines the series, IEC 60063, is a paid document that we did not read.

Choosing a value

  1. Calculate the value you need.
  2. Find the standard values just below and above it.
  3. If the circuit is sensitive in one direction (an LED resistor, for example), choose the value on the safe side: for an LED it is the value above, which gives less current.

Our calculators for LED resistors and dividers do this for you, and the page on series and parallel resistors shows how to make an in-between value from two resistors.

Why 4.7 and not 4.6?

4.7 is in the series. 4.6 is not.

Are there finer series?

Yes: E48, E96 and E192 exist for 1% and finer parts. This site lists E12 and E24.
