The four formulas

Ohm's law links voltage V (volts), current I (amperes) and resistance R (ohms): V = I × R. Power P (watts) is P = V × I. From these two, every pair of values gives the other two:

Typing values

You may use the usual prefixes: k (thousand), M (million), m (milli, a thousandth), u (micro, a millionth), n (nano) and p (pico). 4k7 means 4.7 kilo-ohm, and 20m means 0.02. Units such as V, A or the ohm sign at the end are ignored.

How it was tested

We gave the calculator 1,200 random pairs of values and checked that, in every answer, V equals I × R and P equals V × I, and that the two values you typed were returned unchanged.

Why do I get an error when I fill three boxes?

Two values fix the circuit. A third could disagree with them. Clear one.

Does it work for AC?

For a resistor, with RMS values, yes. Coils and capacitors need impedance, not only resistance.
