Domain status codes explained

The status values show locks, holds and the stage of a domain's life.

The common statuses

ICANN's page on EPP status codes explains them. In short:

StatusMeaning
ok / activeThe domain is registered and in normal use
client transfer prohibitedThe registrar has locked the domain against transfer to another registrar
client delete prohibitedThe registrar has locked it against deletion
client update prohibitedThe registrar has locked it against changes
server transfer / delete / update prohibitedThe same locks, set by the registry
client hold / server holdThe domain is not activated in the DNS, so it does not resolve
inactiveNo name servers are set, so the domain does not resolve
redemption periodThe domain was deleted or expired and can still be restored by its owner, usually for a fee
pending deleteThe domain is going to be deleted

ICANN says that the three client locks (transfer, delete, update) can help to prevent unauthorized transfers, deletions or updates, so seeing them on your domain is normal and good.

What to do

  1. Locks on an active domain: nothing. Ask the registrar to remove the transfer lock only when you want to move.
  2. Hold: contact the registrar. ICANN describes client hold as uncommon and usually linked to legal disputes, non-payment or deletion.
  3. Redemption or pending delete: act at once if the domain is yours: contact the registrar to restore it. Fees often apply.
  4. Inactive: set the name servers at your registrar.

Check

Use the domain lookup page: it lists the statuses of a domain with this kind of explanation. The wording of statuses in RDAP uses spaces ("client transfer prohibited") where EPP uses camel case ("clientTransferProhibited").

Why can I not transfer my domain?

A transfer lock is probably on. Unlock it at your registrar and get the authorization code.

Does expired mean gone?

Not at once. There are grace and redemption periods, but their length varies and the fees are higher.
