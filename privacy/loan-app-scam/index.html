Instant loan app harassment and loan fraud

A lender that needs your contacts and photo gallery, or a fee before it lends, is not a lender you want.

Two kinds of loan fraud

The fee thief. An advertisement or caller offers a quick loan at a low rate with no documents. To release it you must first pay a processing fee, insurance, GST or a "file charge". After you pay, there is another charge, then silence. No loan ever arrives. A genuine lender deducts its charges from the loan; it does not ask you to pay first into a personal account.

The predatory app. You install an instant loan app. It asks for permission to read your contacts, photos, messages and location. A small loan arrives quickly, often less than you applied for after large "charges", repayable in days. Then the calls begin: abuse, threats, and messages to your relatives, colleagues and friends calling you a defaulter or a thief, sometimes with your photo altered. Repaying brings a new "loan" you never asked for and fresh demands.

The signs

  • The app is not linked to a regulated lender. In India, lending is done by banks and by non-banking financial companies registered with the Reserve Bank of India, sometimes through partner apps that must name the regulated lender.
  • It demands access to contacts, gallery and messages. A lender has no need of your photo gallery.
  • A fee before the loan.
  • Very short repayment periods and charges that swallow much of the loan.
  • No clear company name, address or grievance officer.
  • You were reached by an unknown number or a social media advertisement.

Before you borrow

  1. Check the lender's name. The app or website should state clearly which bank or registered NBFC gives the loan; check that company's own website for the list of its lending apps.
  2. Read the permissions the app asks for, and refuse if they go beyond what a loan needs.
  3. Read the key facts: amount you will receive, total you will repay, the date, and every charge.
  4. If you need a small loan, ask your own bank first.

If you are being harassed

  • Do not pay more than you actually owe, and do not take a new loan to close the old one.
  • Keep evidence: screenshots of threats and messages, call records, the app's name and its page in the store, loan and payment details.
  • Warn your contacts briefly that a fraudulent app has stolen your contact list and may send them false messages. Shame loses its power once it is expected.
  • Remove the app's access: uninstall it and revoke its permissions. See Android privacy settings.
  • Report it on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in; threats, obscene images and extortion are crimes. If money was taken by fraud, dial 1930.
  • Where the lender is a regulated company, you can also complain to its grievance officer and, if that fails, under the Reserve Bank of India's ombudsman scheme.
  • If the pressure is affecting your state of mind, talk to someone you trust today. No debt is worth your safety, and this situation has a way out.
They say they will file a police case and arrest me today. Can they?

Not repaying a loan is a civil matter between lender and borrower, handled through notices and courts, not instant arrest. Threats of this kind are intimidation.

Should I pay to stop them messaging my family?

Paying beyond what you owe usually brings more demands. Collect evidence, warn your contacts and report.

If money has already gone

  1. Dial 1930 at once. It is the national helpline for victims of financial cyber fraud. Give the transaction details; the earlier the report, the better the chance that the money can be held before it is moved on.
  2. Call your bank or card company on its official number and block the card, UPI or account. Report the transaction as unauthorised and note the complaint number.
  3. File the complaint on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal, cybercrime.gov.in, and keep the acknowledgement number.
  4. Keep everything: screenshots, numbers, UPI IDs, account numbers, transaction references, links, the app's name. Do not delete chats.
