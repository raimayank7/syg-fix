Five rules that stop most fraud

  1. An OTP, PIN or password is never said aloud or sent to anyone. No bank, police officer, courier or relative needs it.
  2. You never pay, scan or enter a PIN to receive money.
  3. You never install an app or share your screen because a caller asked.
  4. You find phone numbers and websites yourself, from your card, bill or the official app, not from a message or the first search result.
  5. Hurry is the warning. Any message that needs action "today" can wait ten minutes while you ask someone.

Read them to the elders and the teenagers at home. Most victims knew the rules; the fraudster simply got them to break one in a moment of fear or hope.

Why this site exists

Online fraud in India no longer depends on technical skill. It depends on a script: a frightening or tempting story, delivered by someone patient, that walks an ordinary person step by step into handing over money or a code. The stories change every few months, but the steps underneath do not. Once you can see the steps, the story loses its grip.

So each page here does three things: shows how a fraud unfolds, lists the signs that give it away, and says exactly what to do next. The official parts, such as helpline numbers and the bank liability rule, are taken from government and Reserve Bank of India websites, with the date we read them (12 November 2026).

Is this a government website?

No. It is an independent information site. It links to the official portals, where reports are actually made.

Can you help me get my money back?

We cannot take or follow up complaints, and nobody can honestly promise recovery. The fastest route is 1930, your bank and the cyber crime portal. Beware of anyone who offers recovery for a fee.

Do the tools on this site collect what I type?

No. The message checker, link checker and password generator run in your browser. Nothing is sent or stored.
