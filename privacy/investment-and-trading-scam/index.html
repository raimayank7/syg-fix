Fake trading apps and investment group scams

If profits are guaranteed and the money goes to a personal account, it is not an investment.

How it works

  1. The door. An advertisement with a famous investor's face, a message from a "research analyst", or a stranger who chats with you for weeks, invites you to a free group for stock tips.
  2. The classroom. A "professor" and his "assistant" post market lessons and tips. Members thank them and post profit screenshots. Most members are fake accounts.
  3. The app. You are told that big investors use a special "institutional account" or get early access to public issues, through an app or website the group provides. It is not on a regulated exchange, whatever its name suggests.
  4. The deposits. You pay into bank accounts or UPI IDs that change each time and belong to unrelated individuals or small firms. The app shows your money multiplying.
  5. The lock. A small early withdrawal may be allowed. A large one needs "tax", "commission" or a bigger deposit to "unlock" a share allotment. Then the app stops opening.

The same pattern is used with cryptocurrency, foreign exchange, gold and "AI trading bots".

The signs

  • Guaranteed or very high returns. Real markets guarantee nothing.
  • You are asked to trade through an app or link the group gives you, instead of your own demat account with a registered broker.
  • Payment to a personal or unrelated account, by UPI or bank transfer, with a new account each time.
  • Pressure: limited seats, an offer closing tonight, the assistant messaging you privately.
  • Withdrawals need more deposits.
  • A celebrity video recommending the scheme. Such clips are often faked.
  • A stranger who became a friend online and then turned the talk to investing.

How to check before you invest

  • In India, stock brokers, investment advisers and research analysts must be registered with SEBI. Check the name and registration number on SEBI's own website, and check that the bank account you are paying into belongs to that registered entity.
  • Shares are bought only through a demat and trading account in your own name with a registered broker. Money moves between your own bank account and the broker, never to a third person.
  • Search the name of the app with the word "fraud" or "complaint".
  • Take a day. A real opportunity survives a night's sleep and a talk with someone you trust.

If you are already in

  • Stop paying, whatever is shown as locked. Do not pay "tax" or "fees" to withdraw: taxes are never paid to an app to release money.
  • Take screenshots of the app, the group, the profiles, all account numbers and UPI IDs, and every transaction reference.
  • Report quickly. See "If money has already gone" below.
  • Expect a follow-up scam: "lawyers" or "cyber cell officers" who promise recovery for an advance fee. Recovery, where it happens, comes through the police and your bank, free of charge.
The app shows my balance has doubled. Is the money real?

The screen is controlled by the fraudsters and can show any number. Money is real only when it is back in your own bank account.

A group member I trust withdrew money successfully. Should I continue?

Small withdrawals are allowed on purpose, to keep everyone depositing. They prove nothing.

If money has already gone

  1. Dial 1930 at once. It is the national helpline for victims of financial cyber fraud. Give the transaction details; the earlier the report, the better the chance that the money can be held before it is moved on.
  2. Call your bank or card company on its official number and block the card, UPI or account. Report the transaction as unauthorised and note the complaint number.
  3. File the complaint on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal, cybercrime.gov.in, and keep the acknowledgement number.
  4. Keep everything: screenshots, numbers, UPI IDs, account numbers, transaction references, links, the app's name. Do not delete chats.
