Cron expression for every 12 hours

Every 12 hours, on the hour, every day.

Five fields for Linux, GitHub Actions and Kubernetes (minute hour day month weekday). Six or seven for Quartz. Nothing you type leaves your device.

What it means and when to use it

Twice a day is a natural rhythm for backups, for rotating logs and for a "morning and evening" report. */12 in the hours field takes hours 0 and 12, so the job runs at 00:00 and 12:00 server time. Writing 0 0,12 * * * gives the same result and is easier for a beginner to read.

Watch out for this

Midnight and noon are exactly the moments many other jobs choose, and the clock change on daylight-saving day can also shift what "midnight" means in a local zone. If you can, choose off-peak minutes such as 20 0,12 * * *. If you need two specific times that are not 12 hours apart, such as 08:30 and 20:15, they cannot be written in one line with a step: use two lines, one for each time.

The same schedule in other places

Quartz

Quartz has a seconds field in front, uses ? in one day field, and counts Sunday as 1:

0 0 */12 * * ?

GitHub Actions

Scheduled workflows run in UTC unless you add a time zone. In the workflow file:

on:
  schedule:
    - cron: '0 */12 * * *'

Kubernetes CronJob

In a CronJob manifest the schedule goes in spec.schedule. Without spec.timeZone, it is read in the time zone of the cluster's controller:

spec:
  schedule: "0 */12 * * *"
  timeZone: "Etc/UTC"

A crontab line to copy

The command is made up. Replace it with your own and use full paths:

0 0,12 * * * /home/me/bin/backup.sh >> /home/me/backup.log 2>&1

Questions

Is @daily the same as running every 12 hours?

No. @daily is once a day at midnight. Use two hours in the hour field for twice a day.
