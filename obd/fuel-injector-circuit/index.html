P0200 to P0212

Fuel injector circuits

Fault codes P0200 to P0212 explained in plain words: what the car is telling you, how serious it is and what to check first.

How serious: MediumDrive gently and have it repaired soon. Leaving it can lead to bigger faults or higher fuel use.

What it means

Each cylinder has an injector, an electric valve that sprays fuel. These codes are about the electrical circuit of an injector: the computer sees an open circuit, a short, or a current that is wrong. The last digits give the cylinder number.

Bank 1 is the side of the engine that has cylinder 1. Sensor 1 is before the catalytic converter and sensor 2 is after it. More terms explained.

What you may notice

  • Engine shaking, as one cylinder is not working
  • Loss of power
  • Misfire codes for the same cylinder
  • Fuel smell if an injector is stuck open

Common causes

Most likely first:

  • A loose or corroded injector plug
  • Broken wiring to the injector, often from engine movement or rodents
  • A failed injector coil
  • A fault inside the engine computer (rare)

What to check first

  • Check the plug on the injector for the cylinder named
  • Look for chewed or chafed wires on top of the engine
  • A workshop can swap two injectors to see if the fault follows the injector

Have a mechanic confirm. A fault code tells you which system reported a problem, not which part has failed. The same code can have several causes, and they differ between car models. Use this page to understand the code, then have a qualified mechanic test the car before any part is replaced.

After the repair

Once the cause is fixed, the code can be cleared with a scan tool, or the warning light goes out by itself after several trips without the fault. Clearing a code without fixing the cause only hides it for a while. More about clearing codes.
