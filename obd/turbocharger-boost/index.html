P0234 to P0299

Turbocharger and boost pressure

Fault codes P0234 to P0299 explained in plain words: what the car is telling you, how serious it is and what to check first.

How serious: MediumDrive gently and have it repaired soon. Leaving it can lead to bigger faults or higher fuel use.

What it means

A turbocharger pushes extra air into the engine. The computer controls how much pressure, or boost, it makes. These codes mean the boost is higher or lower than commanded, or a sensor or control valve in the boost system has an electrical fault.

Bank 1 is the side of the engine that has cylinder 1. Sensor 1 is before the catalytic converter and sensor 2 is after it. More terms explained.

What you may notice

  • Clear loss of power (underboost) or surging (overboost)
  • Whistling or hissing under acceleration
  • Limp mode
  • Black smoke on a diesel

Common causes

Most likely first:

  • A split or loose intercooler hose or clamp
  • A sticking wastegate or variable-vane mechanism, often from soot
  • A leaking or failed boost control valve or its vacuum hoses
  • A faulty boost pressure sensor
  • A worn turbocharger

What to check first

  • Look along the large air hoses from turbo to intercooler to engine for oil mist, splits or a popped clamp
  • Listen for a new hiss when accelerating
  • Drive gently: hard use with a boost fault can damage the turbo or engine

Have a mechanic confirm. A fault code tells you which system reported a problem, not which part has failed. The same code can have several causes, and they differ between car models. Use this page to understand the code, then have a qualified mechanic test the car before any part is replaced.

After the repair

Once the cause is fixed, the code can be cleared with a scan tool, or the warning light goes out by itself after several trips without the fault. Clearing a code without fixing the cause only hides it for a while. More about clearing codes.
